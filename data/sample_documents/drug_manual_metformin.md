# Drug Reference Manual: Metformin Hydrochloride

**Drug Class:** Biguanide, oral antihyperglycemic
**Brand Names:** Glucophage, Fortamet, Glumetza
**Manual Section:** 4.2 — Endocrine Agents

## Indications

Metformin is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve
glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients (age 10+) with type 2
diabetes mellitus. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with
other antihyperglycemic agents, including insulin.

## Dosage and Administration

**Adults — Immediate Release:**
- Initial: 500 mg orally twice daily, or 850 mg once daily, with meals
- Titration: increase by 500 mg weekly, or 850 mg every 2 weeks, based
  on glycemic response and tolerability
- Maximum: 2550 mg/day in divided doses

**Adults — Extended Release:**
- Initial: 500-1000 mg orally once daily with the evening meal
- Titration: increase by 500 mg weekly
- Maximum: 2000 mg/day (2500 mg/day for Fortamet)

**Renal Impairment (eGFR-based dosing):**
- eGFR ≥ 45 mL/min/1.73m²: no dose adjustment required
- eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²: use with caution; do not initiate new therapy; max 1000 mg/day
- eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²: contraindicated

## Contraindications

- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²)
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin

## Warnings and Precautions

**Lactic Acidosis (Boxed Warning):** Metformin can cause lactic
acidosis, a rare but serious metabolic complication. Risk increases
with renal impairment, sepsis, dehydration, excessive alcohol intake,
hepatic impairment, and acute congestive heart failure. Discontinue
immediately if lactic acidosis is suspected; symptoms include malaise,
myalgia, respiratory distress, and abdominal distress.

**Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** Long-term use may result in decreased
vitamin B12 absorption. Monitor B12 levels periodically, particularly
in patients with anemia or peripheral neuropathy.

**Iodinated Contrast Media:** Discontinue metformin at the time of, or
prior to, an imaging procedure using iodinated contrast in patients
with eGFR 30-60 mL/min/1.73m², hepatic disease, or history of alcohol
use. Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours after the procedure before resuming.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Iodinated contrast media | Increased risk of lactic acidosis | Temporary discontinuation, see above |
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (e.g., topiramate) | Additive risk of lactic acidosis | Monitor closely |
| Cimetidine | Increased metformin plasma concentration | Consider dose adjustment |
| Alcohol | Potentiates effect on lactate metabolism | Advise caution/avoidance |

## Adverse Reactions

Common (>5%): diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, asthenia,
indigestion, abdominal discomfort, headache.

Serious but rare: lactic acidosis (see Boxed Warning above).

## Patient Counseling Points

- Take with meals to reduce GI upset
- Report muscle pain, difficulty breathing, or unusual fatigue immediately
- Do not stop or start without consulting your physician, particularly
  around procedures involving contrast dye
