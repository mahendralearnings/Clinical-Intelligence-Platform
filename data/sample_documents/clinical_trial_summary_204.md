# Clinical Trial Summary: Protocol CIP-204

**Title:** A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled
Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Investigational Agent
CIP-204X in Adult Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Rheumatoid Arthritis

**Phase:** III
**Status:** Completed
**Sponsor:** Internal R&D Division
**Study Duration:** 18 months (enrollment) + 12 months (follow-up)

## Background and Rationale

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) affects approximately 1.3 million adults in
the United States, causing chronic joint inflammation, pain, and
progressive disability. While existing biologic DMARDs (disease-
modifying antirheumatic drugs) have improved outcomes significantly, a
meaningful subset of patients — estimated at 30-40% — fail to achieve
adequate response or lose efficacy over time. CIP-204X is a novel
selective JAK1 inhibitor designed to modulate the inflammatory cascade
with a differentiated safety profile compared to first-generation JAK
inhibitors, particularly regarding cardiovascular and thromboembolic
risk signals identified in prior class-wide studies.

## Study Design

Eligible participants (n=642) were randomized 1:1:1 to receive
CIP-204X 100mg once daily, CIP-204X 200mg once daily, or placebo, in
addition to stable background methotrexate therapy. Randomization was
stratified by baseline disease activity score (DAS28-CRP) and prior
biologic exposure. The study employed a double-blind design with
matching placebo tablets to maintain treatment concealment through
Week 24, after which placebo non-responders were re-randomized to
active treatment in a blinded extension phase.

## Primary Endpoint

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving ACR20
response (American College of Rheumatology 20% improvement criteria)
at Week 12. Secondary endpoints included ACR50/ACR70 response rates,
change from baseline in DAS28-CRP, and Health Assessment Questionnaire
Disability Index (HAQ-DI) scores at Weeks 12 and 24.

## Key Efficacy Results

At Week 12, ACR20 response was achieved by 68.4% of patients in the
CIP-204X 200mg arm, 61.2% in the 100mg arm, and 32.7% in the placebo
arm (p<0.001 for both active arms versus placebo). ACR50 response
rates followed a similar pattern, with 41.3% response in the 200mg
arm compared to 14.8% in placebo. Improvements in DAS28-CRP were
observed as early as Week 2 in both active treatment arms, with
continued separation from placebo through Week 24.

## Safety Summary

The overall safety profile was consistent with the JAK inhibitor
class, with the most common treatment-emergent adverse events being
upper respiratory tract infection, nausea, and headache. Serious
infections occurred in 2.1% of the combined active treatment group
versus 1.4% in placebo. Two cases of deep vein thrombosis were
reported in the 200mg arm; both patients had pre-existing risk
factors (obesity and prior VTE history). No cases of pulmonary
embolism or major adverse cardiovascular events were reported during
the study period. Herpes zoster reactivation occurred in 3.4% of
active-treatment patients versus 0.5% in placebo, consistent with
known class effects.

## Conclusions and Next Steps

CIP-204X demonstrated statistically significant and clinically
meaningful improvement in RA disease activity at both dose levels
compared to placebo, with a safety profile generally consistent with
the JAK inhibitor class. The 200mg dose showed numerically greater
efficacy without a proportional increase in serious safety signals,
though the observed thromboembolic events warrant continued monitoring
in the ongoing long-term extension study (CIP-204-EXT). These results
support submission of a New Drug Application, pending finalization of
the 12-month safety follow-up data.
