# SOP-014: Adverse Event Reporting and Escalation

**Document Type:** Standard Operating Procedure
**Version:** 3.2
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-15
**Owner:** Clinical Compliance Department
**Applies To:** All clinical trial sites, Clinical Operations staff, Investigators

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the process for identifying, documenting, and reporting
adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) occurring during
clinical trials sponsored by the organization, in accordance with FDA
21 CFR 312.32 and ICH E2A guidelines.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all personnel involved in clinical trial
conduct, including Principal Investigators, Clinical Research
Coordinators, and Clinical Operations staff, across all active
protocols.

## 3. Definitions

3.1. **Adverse Event (AE):** Any untoward medical occurrence in a
patient administered a pharmaceutical product, which does not
necessarily have a causal relationship with the treatment.

3.2. **Serious Adverse Event (SAE):** An AE that results in death, is
life-threatening, requires inpatient hospitalization, results in
persistent disability, or is a congenital anomaly.

3.3. **Unexpected Adverse Event:** An AE not listed in the current
Investigator's Brochure or not listed at the specificity/severity
observed.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identification and Initial Documentation

Step 1: The Investigator or delegate identifies a potential AE during
a scheduled visit, unscheduled visit, or through participant-initiated
contact.

Step 2: The event is documented in the source document within 24 hours
of identification, including onset date, description, severity grade
(per CTCAE v5.0), and relationship to study drug.

Step 3: The AE is entered into the Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system
within 3 business days.

### 4.2 Serious Adverse Event Escalation

Step 1: If the event meets SAE criteria (see Section 3.2), the
Investigator must notify the Sponsor's Pharmacovigilance department
within 24 hours of first knowledge, regardless of whether the event is
expected or related.

Step 2: A completed SAE Report Form must be submitted within 24 hours
of initial notification. Follow-up reports are required for any change
in patient status until resolution.

Step 3: For SAEs deemed both serious AND unexpected AND possibly
related to study drug, the Sponsor's Regulatory Affairs department
submits an expedited report to FDA within 15 calendar days (7 calendar
days if fatal or life-threatening), per 21 CFR 312.32(c).

### 4.3 Institutional Review Board (IRB) Notification

Step 1: The Investigator reports all SAEs to the site's IRB per local
IRB timelines, typically within 10 working days, or per local
requirements if more stringent.

## 5. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Investigator | Identify, document, and report AEs/SAEs within required timelines |
| Clinical Research Coordinator | Assist with documentation and EDC entry |
| Pharmacovigilance | Triage, causality assessment, expedited reporting |
| Regulatory Affairs | Submit expedited reports to FDA and other authorities |
